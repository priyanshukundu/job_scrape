The Opportunity Abbott's diagnostics digital product organization is seeking a Principal AI Solutions Architect to define and advance the technical strategy for embedding artificial intelligence into digital products across the laboratory medicine workflow. This individual will serve as a senior technical authority for AI-enabled solutions spanning laboratory pre-analytics, analytics, post-analytics, workflow optimization, and clinical decision support. The Principal AI Solutions Architect will establish the reference architectures, data strategies, engineering standards, and implementation approaches needed to deliver safe, reliable, scalable, and differentiated AI capabilities. This is a senior individual-contributor role for a leader who can connect emerging AI capabilities with real laboratory and healthcare needs. The successful candidate will combine deep technical expertise with product judgment, architecture leadership, and hands-on engineering. This individual will work across product management, systems engineering, software engineering, data engineering, clinical and medical affairs, cybersecurity, regulatory, quality, and other functions to translate complex opportunities into production-ready solutions. The role requires more than evaluating AI technologies or developing proofs of concept. It requires ownership of the technical direction and critical decisions needed to move AI-enabled capabilities from concept through architecture, implementation, evaluation, product integration, deployment, monitoring, and lifecycle management. What You’ll Work On AI Technical Strategy and Architecture Define and drive the long-term AI technical strategy, target architecture, and technology roadmap for Abbott's digital laboratory products. Establish reference architectures, technical standards, reusable patterns, and shared platform capabilities for AI-enabled products. Translate product, laboratory, clinical, scientific, and operational needs into scalable AI architectures and executable implementation strategies. Provide technical direction for AI capabilities across laboratory pre-analytics, analytics, post-analytics, workflow optimization, clinical decision support, and related digital-health applications. Define architectural approaches spanning applications, data, models, integration, cloud and edge infrastructure, cybersecurity, privacy, observability, and governance. Lead architectural decisions involving predictive machine learning, natural-language processing, generative AI, large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, conversational AI, agent-based workflows, knowledge-based systems, and optimization methods. Determine when AI, deterministic software, rules-based logic, statistical methods, or combined approaches are most appropriate for a product use case. Serve as a senior technical escalation point for complex, ambiguous, cross-product AI architecture and implementation challenges. Data and Model Strategy Define data strategies that support the development, validation, deployment, and continuous monitoring of AI-enabled product capabilities. Establish architectural patterns for data acquisition, preparation, quality, lineage, labeling, access, privacy, retention, and fitness for intended use. Guide decisions concerning enterprise and product data platforms, Lakehouse architectures, feature management, streaming data, vector and graph-based retrieval, and structured and unstructured data. Define approaches for model selection, build-versus-buy analysis, foundation-model selection, model hosting, third-party AI services, and vendor evaluation. Ensure that model and data strategies address product requirements for accuracy, robustness, latency, scalability, interoperability, security, cost, traceability, and lifecycle maintainability. Establish approaches for evaluating model performance across representative populations, workflows, operating conditions, and foreseeable failure modes. Guide the use of human oversight, confidence thresholds, fallback behavior, escalation pathways, and other controls appropriate to the intended use and risk of each capability. Productization and AI Lifecycle Engineering Define engineering practices for moving AI capabilities from experimentation and prototyping into production-grade digital products. Establish MLOps and LLMOps practices covering data and model versioning, prompt and configuration management, automated evaluation, CI/CD, deployment, observability, rollback, and incident response. Define evaluation strategies and acceptance criteria for predictive ML, generative AI, retrieval, conversational, and agent-based systems. Establish production monitoring approaches for model quality, drift, grounding, retrieval performance, latency, reliability, cost, security, and unexpected system behavior. Guide the design of AI services, APIs, event-driven integrations, data pipelines, orchestration workflows, and reusable platform components. Lead architecture proofs, critical prototypes, design reviews, technical investigations, and resolution of high-risk implementation problems. Ensure AI-enabled solutions are designed for maintainability, testability, reliability, cybersecurity, interoperability, and operational support. Guide architectural trade-offs across cloud, on-premises, hybrid, edge, and instrument-connected deployment environments. Responsible AI and Regulated Product Development Define technical approaches for responsible AI, including transparency, explainability, traceability, privacy, cybersecurity, fairness, human oversight, and prevention of unintended behavior. Partner with regulatory, quality, clinical, medical, privacy, legal, and cybersecurity stakeholders to align AI architectures and lifecycle processes with applicable product and regulatory requirements. Support risk analysis by identifying potential AI failure modes, foreseeable misuse, data limitations, model uncertainty, and dependencies on external models or services. Ensure AI architectures support evidence generation, design documentation, verification and validation, change control, and post-deployment monitoring. Establish safeguards for generative and agent-based systems, including grounding, access control, prompt-injection defenses, action boundaries, output validation, auditable decision paths, and human approval where appropriate. Help define governance mechanisms for evaluating proposed AI use cases, models, data sources, platforms, vendors, and changes to production AI solutions. Technical Leadership and Influence Act as a trusted technical advisor to senior product, engineering, business, clinical, and executive leaders. Present AI strategies, architecture alternatives, investment options, technical risks, and recommendations in a clear and decision-oriented manner. Influence product and technology roadmaps by identifying where AI can deliver meaningful value and where technical, data, regulatory, or clinical limitations require a different approach. Lead cross-functional architecture and design reviews involving product, systems, software, data, cloud, cybersecurity, clinical, regulatory, quality, and verification teams. Guide build-versus-buy decisions and technical assessments of AI platforms, models, frameworks, service providers, and development partners. Mentor Staff, Senior, and other engineers, strengthening architecture and AI-engineering capabilities across the organization. Create reusable guidance, reference implementations, technical documentation, and knowledge-sharing mechanisms. Represent Abbott's AI technical capabilities with strategic partners, vendors, external experts, and industry organizations as appropriate. Promote responsible use of AI-assisted software-development tools while maintaining human accountability for architecture, design, source code, testing, security, intellectual property, and product-quality decisions.
