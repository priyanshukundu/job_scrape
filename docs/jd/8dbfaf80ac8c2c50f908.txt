Fitch Solutions is currently seeking a Product Owner, Bankruptcy Intelligence based out of our New York office. Fitch Solutions is a leading provider of insights, data and analytics. It informs investment strategies, strengthens risk management capabilities and helps identify strategic opportunities. Its analysts, lawyers, journalists and economists offer in-depth views on credit markets/risk and individual credits, ESG, developed and emerging markets, and industry sectors. Fitch Solutions is part of Fitch Group, a global leader in financial information services with operations in over 30 countries. Fitch Group is owned by Hearst. By becoming a part of the Fitch Solutions team, you will join a group of colleagues delivering critical data, insightful research, and comprehensive analytics that empower clients to make informed decisions. You’ll work in a dynamic environment where innovation is encouraged, and collaboration is key to developing solutions that address the evolving needs of global markets. With a portfolio of best-in-class, award winning brands, we offer you the opportunity to advance your career while contributing to a company known for its expertise and commitment to excellence. About the Role Restructuring professionals rely on court filings, news reports, docket data, advisor disclosures, and recovery analyses to make time-sensitive decisions on distressed credits. Most of that data is unstructured, scattered across PACER and court systems, and manually tracked in spreadsheets. We’re building the product that changes that. This Product Owner will own the backlog and delivery for Bankruptcy Intelligence, a data centric platform combining PacerMonitor’s court docket infrastructure with CreditSights’ editorial depth and a structured bankruptcy database covering hundreds of corporate Chapter 11 cases comprising of hundreds of thousands of filings. You’ll manage a squad, work closely with the product manager who owns the strategy, and make the daily calls on what gets built next. Capacity will be tight and every sprint choice will have a visible trade-off. The right person for this role is comfortable with that constraint and knows how to sequence work so the highest-value capabilities ship first. How You’ll Make an Impact: Own the Backlog and Ship Translate the product roadmap into epics, user stories, and acceptance criteria. Own sprint planning, backlog sequencing, and release coordination with engineering. You are the person who decides what goes into each sprint and defends those choices when priorities shift. Build and Scale a Structured Bankruptcy Dataset The bankruptcy data that clients need, case attributes, advisor networks, creditor disclosures, recovery outcomes lives in court filings and legacy formats. You’ll own the product decisions that determine how that data gets extracted, structured, validated, and kept current. This includes defining accuracy thresholds, managing data quality workflows, and expanding coverage as the dataset grows. Drive AI into the Data Pipeline Court documents are high-volume and unstructured. Manual processing doesn’t scale. You’ll work with engineering to define where AI accelerates the pipeline: automated docket classification, entity extraction from filings, structured data generation from Rule 2019 disclosures, and document summarization. Your job is to identify which extraction problems are solvable with AI today and which still need human review, then build the workflow that combines both. Coordinate Across Product, Editorial, and Engineering You’ll run UAT cycles, manage stakeholder feedback from restructuring professionals, and coordinate across PacerMonitor, CreditSights editorial, and engineering to ensure what ships matches what clients use. What Makes This Role Different Most PO roles hand you a well-defined product with an established data model. This one doesn’t. The dataset is being built from raw court filings and legacy sources. The squad is lean. The decisions you make on what to extract, how to structure it, and where to apply AI will shape whether the product closes the gap on coverage and speed. The PO here shapes the data architecture, not just the feature list.
