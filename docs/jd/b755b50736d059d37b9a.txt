The IT Portfolio Lead is responsible for managing the integrated portfolio of technology investments across Wholesale & Specialty, ensuring IT capacity and investment are consistently directed toward the highest-value business priorities. The role provides enterprise-level transparency across demand, prioritization, investment, capacity, delivery, dependencies, and realized benefits. Working across business leaders, Product Managers, Product Owners, technology leaders, Finance, and delivery teams, the IT Portfolio Lead establishes the portfolio management discipline required to translate strategy into an executable and economically sound technology investment portfolio. This position does not own individual products or projects. Instead, it ensures that the overall portfolio is balanced, prioritized, funded, executable, and delivering the business outcomes expected from technology investment. Key Responsibilities Portfolio Strategy & Planning Maintain the integrated IT portfolio across value streams, strategic programs, regulatory commitments, technology modernization, and operational investments. Translate business and technology strategies into a prioritized portfolio and investment roadmap. Facilitate quarterly and annual portfolio planning and investment discussions. Ensure proposed initiatives have clearly defined business outcomes, sponsorship, costs, capacity requirements, dependencies, and expected benefits. Maintain visibility into the balance of investment across growth and increased GWP; product and forms capability; underwriter and broker experience; claims outcomes; expense reduction; operational capacity creation; risk, regulatory, and compliance requirements; and technology modernization and technical debt. Demand & Prioritization Own the enterprise IT demand intake and prioritization framework. Establish consistent criteria for comparing investments across value streams and business areas. Facilitate prioritization using strategic alignment, financial value, customer or underwriter impact, risk, regulatory necessity, delivery complexity, dependencies, and capacity. Challenge requests that lack measurable outcomes or appropriate business sponsorship. Provide leadership with clear recommendations regarding what should be accelerated, deferred, stopped, or re-scoped. Maintain transparency into the opportunity cost associated with portfolio decisions. Investment & Benefit Management Partner with Finance and business sponsors to establish credible investment cases for significant technology initiatives. Establish expected benefits before investment approval, including GWP or revenue growth, expense reduction, capacity gain, loss-cost or claims improvement, risk reduction, and service or experience improvement. Establish benefit owners, measurement approaches, baselines, and realization timelines. Track expected versus realized benefits and escalate material variances. Ensure capacity benefits are translated into explicit harvesting plans, such as redeployment, avoided hiring, increased throughput, or expense reduction. Portfolio Capacity Management Maintain an enterprise view of IT capacity and allocation across value streams and major initiatives. Create visibility into capacity consumed by run/support, regulatory and mandatory work, product enhancement, growth initiatives, strategic transformation, and technical debt and modernization. Identify portfolio bottlenecks, scarce capabilities, vendor constraints, and competing resource demands. Partner with technology leadership to recommend capacity shifts based on business priorities. Incorporate internal, offshore, and strategic vendor capacity into the portfolio view. Portfolio Governance Establish a lightweight, decision-oriented portfolio governance process. Prepare and facilitate portfolio reviews with business and technology leadership. Maintain a clear record of portfolio decisions, priorities, dependencies, risks, and investment trade-offs. Define escalation paths for cross-value-stream conflicts and resource constraints. Ensure governance enables timely decisions rather than becoming an administrative approval layer. Portfolio Performance & Executive Transparency Develop a concise executive portfolio view showing investment by value stream and business outcome; IT capacity allocation; portfolio health; delivery confidence; major milestones; investment consumed versus approved; expected versus realized benefits; cross-portfolio dependencies; key risks and decisions required; and initiatives recommended to start, stop, accelerate, defer, or re-scope. Move portfolio reporting away from activity measures toward business outcomes and investment effectiveness. Cross-Functional Leadership Serve as the connection point among business executives, Product Management, Product Owners, Finance, Technology, Architecture, Change Management, and delivery leadership. Resolve competing priorities across business units and value streams through fact-based portfolio analysis. Ensure Product Owners and delivery teams understand portfolio priorities while retaining ownership of product backlogs and execution decisions. Partner with Architecture and technology leadership so portfolio decisions account for strategic platforms, integration, data, security, AI enablement, and technical sustainability. Coordinate with Change Management to understand the organization's capacity to absorb significant business change. Qualifications 8+ years of experience across technology portfolio management, technology strategy, product management, transformation, program leadership, or related disciplines. Experience managing investment portfolios in a complex, multi-business environment. Strong understanding of technology economics, capacity planning, portfolio prioritization, and investment governance. Demonstrated ability to translate technology activity into financial and operational business outcomes. Experience establishing executive-level portfolio reporting and decision frameworks. Strong financial acumen with experience working with budgets, business cases, benefits, and investment trade-offs. Ability to influence senior business and technology leaders without relying on direct organizational authority. Strong facilitation, analytical, and executive communication skills. Experience in commercial, specialty, or wholesale insurance strongly preferred. Preferred Experience Wholesale and specialty insurance operations and underwriting workflows Product-centric or value-stream technology operating models Agile product delivery Technology financial management Vendor and offshore delivery models Cloud and platform modernization Data, automation, and AI investment portfolios Benefit realization and capacity-harvesting disciplines Leadership Attributes Thinks in terms of business value rather than project activity. Is financially disciplined and comfortable challenging weak investment cases. Understands both business strategy and technology delivery. Uses data to create transparency and drive decisions. Can distinguish strategically important work from organizational noise. Is comfortable making trade-offs visible to senior leadership. Creates governance that speeds decisions rather than creating bureaucracy.
