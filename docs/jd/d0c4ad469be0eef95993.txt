Job Description General Motors is a global leader in advanced driver assistance. With Super Cruise hands-free technology in more than 500,000 Super Cruise–equipped vehicles on the road and over 700 million hands-free miles driven, GM is proving that automation can be trusted, intuitive, and helpful. GM has the global reach to bring cutting-edge advances to everyday drivers at unprecedented scale. Join us to help deliver the next generation of safe and delightful personal autonomous vehicle experiences. About the Team Every autonomous-driving engineer at GM eventually asks the same questions: What happened on that drive? Where else has this happened? Why did the vehicle do that? Did my change fix it? The AV Analysis team builds the tools that answer them, across the full life of AV data: Record and catalog new data as it comes in from the fleet, so engineers know what exists, where it came from, and whether it's ready to use. Index and mine road data alongside simulation output, so engineers can find the scenes that matter using structured filters, similarity search, and natural-language queries. Analyze what happened by replaying sensor data, perception output, and planner decisions in synchronized 3D, camera, plot, and map views. Engineers do this to triage and diagnose an issue, or to validate a new change. Perception, planning, simulation, mapping, validation, and data teams use these tools every day. That work spans next-generation autonomous development vehicles, Super Cruise programs, and autonomous mobile robots in GM factories. We're part of AV Tooling, which also builds GM's fleet-operations tools and AI-assisted triage. We share one goal: shorten the loop from collecting data, to understanding it, to fixing the vehicle. About the Role Our senior engineers don't wait for a spec. Demand for our tools outpaces any roadmap, so the most valuable thing you'll do is decide what to build. That means finding the problem that's quietly costing engineers hours every week, designing the tool that removes it, and shipping it. You'll own a major part of the data lifecycle end to end, such as how engineers search and mine data, or how they analyze and debug it. You'll also help connect cataloging, search, and analysis into one workflow. What You'll Do Find the highest-leverage gaps in how engineers find, analyze, and debug AV data. Work alongside users, read support channels and bug reports, and notice where teams are building their own workarounds. Then turn those gaps into tools people adopt. Record and catalog new AV data as it arrives, with the context engineers need to trust and use it: vehicle, software version, location, and ingestion status. Index and mine road data alongside simulation output. Build search that combines structured metadata queries in SQL with embedding-based similarity search in vector databases, plus natural-language queries and LLM- and VLM-powered curation. Build high-performance tools for analyzing what happened, with synchronized replay of camera video, lidar, 3D scenes, plots, and maps. Engineers use them to triage and diagnose issues, to validate new software changes, and to debug live in the vehicle during testing. Take ambiguous requests from idea to shipped. Write requirements and design docs that weigh options, build consensus with stakeholders, and deliver iteratively so users are unblocked early. Serve as technical lead for a multi-project workstream. Plan and break down the work, delegate, hold a high bar in design and code reviews, and guide engineers across GM who contribute to our tools. Keep tools fast and reliable in production, and raise the bar for the team's engineering systems: tests, CI speed, developer experience, observability, and on-call. Mentor engineers, and use AI-assisted development and agentic workflows to multiply what a small team can deliver. Comfort working across the stack, designing APIs and building services in Node.js or Python. Deep experience diagnosing and fixing web application performance problems (rendering, memory, large-data and streaming throughput) through profiling and measurement. Clear written and verbal communication with technical users whose needs differ widely, from perception researchers to test operators. Bachelor's degree in Computer Science, Engineering, or a related field, or equivalent practical experience.
