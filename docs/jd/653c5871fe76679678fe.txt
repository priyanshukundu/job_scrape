Visit gomotive.com to learn more. About the role Every device we ship starts with a forecast. We're hiring a Director to own how we forecast product demand, from the topline view of the business down to the units we need to build and stock. This is a senior, hands-on role for a sharp, curious problem solver. Our data is rich but complex, spread across many systems and broken down by product, region, channel, and customer segment. Both new products and existing products entering new markets launch with little or no history. You'll set the strategy and do the work yourself, using AI and automation to get the reach that would normally take a team. You'll be the senior forecasting partner to Finance, Sales, Product, and Supply Chain leadership, and your numbers will drive what we build, buy, and stock. You don't need to be a supply chain expert. We care more about how you think than about years spent in operations. If you're strong at forecasting, great with complex data, and learn fast, we'll help you learn the supply chain side. What you'll do Own the topline product forecast. Build and be accountable for the forecast by product line, connecting pipeline, bookings, and subscription trends to the hardware units those deals require. Set the forecasting strategy. Define how we forecast and plan across the company: the process, cadence, systems, and tooling roadmap (including connected planning platforms such as Pigment). Forecast new product introductions. Own launch forecasts for products with no sales history, using analogs, pipeline signals, pricing, and adoption assumptions. Plan cannibalization, transitions from legacy products, and end-of-life ramps. Forecast every product in every market. Build forecasts for each product as it enters new geographies and segments, where adoption curves, pricing, sales channels, competition, and regulatory requirements can differ sharply from markets we already know. Decide when to borrow from established markets and when local signals should take over. Translate forecast into unit demand. Break the topline down accurately by SKU, region, and channel, and keep it current as the business changes. Connect complex data into one view. Bring together detailed data from multiple systems (CRM, ERP, shipments, activations) into a consistent picture of demand. Partner with data and systems owners to strengthen how that data flows into forecasting. Scale with automation. Build repeatable pipelines, scripts, and AI-driven workflows so the forecast refreshes quickly and reliably as the business grows. Turn demand into inventory plans. Partner with Supply Chain on build plans, safety stock, and weeks-of-supply targets, balancing the cost of running out against the cost of holding too much. Lead the monthly demand review. Present scenarios, risks, and assumption changes to executives, and drive alignment on a single demand plan. Own forecast performance. Set accuracy and bias targets, report on them, explain misses, and keep improving the process. What you need 8+ years in forecasting, demand planning, FP&A, analytics, or a similar quantitative role. You pick up new business models and processes quickly and are energized by figuring out how things work. Skill with complex, disaggregated data. A track record of pulling together detailed data from multiple sources, reconciling it at different levels of granularity, and turning it into forecasts leaders trust. Strong SQL or a comparable tool. You've built processes that run without manual rebuilding each cycle. Experience forecasting new products or new markets where history is thin, choosing sensible analogs, and adjusting quickly as real launch data comes in. You use AI tools (LLMs, coding assistants, agents) every day as your default way of working: to write and debug code, clean data, build models, and draft analysis. You can show concrete examples of work AI made faster or better, and you know how to check AI output before trusting it. You understand concepts like lead times, safety stock, and inventory levels, even if you haven't worked in supply chain directly. You've owned an important process end to end and driven decisions across teams without formal authority. You can explain a forecast, its assumptions, and its risks to senior leadership in plain language and defend it under pushback. Nice to have Hands-on experience with Pigment (or another connected planning tool such as Anaplan or Adaptive Planning). Experience in a SaaS business: subscriptions, ARR, attach rates, and how bookings translate into device shipments. Prior n ew product forecasting at a hardware + software company, including launch ramps and legacy product transitions. Experience forecasting international or new-market expansion, where each region has its own adoption pattern, channels, and requirements. What success looks like First 90 days: You understand our products, data sources, and current forecast process. You have a documented baseline forecast, a clear map of the data that feeds it, and a forecasting roadmap agreed with leadership. First 6 months: The monthly demand review runs on a repeatable, largely automated process, and forecast accuracy and bias are reported regularly. First year: Leadership and Supply Chain trust the forecast to drive build and inventory decisions. New products and new-market launches each start with a clear demand plan, accuracy is measurably improving, and the process is highly automated and scales with the business.
