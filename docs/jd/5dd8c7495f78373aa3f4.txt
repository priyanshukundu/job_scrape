Translational Research Scientist – Cancer Biology
Guidehouse
Location: US - MD, Bethesda

Job Family : Scientist Travel Required : None Clearance Required : Ability to Obtain Public Trust What You Will Do: We are looking for a Translational Research Scientist to support the National Cancer Institute (NCI), Center for Cancer Research, Rare Tumor Initiative. This individual will conduct laboratory-based translational cancer research, perform sophisticated molecular, cellular, and animal studies, analyze and interpret research findings, and collaborate with scientific staff to advance cancer diagnostics and therapeutics. This is a full-time position located in Bethesda, MD. Conduct laboratory-based translational research focused on drug development for the treatment of cancer. Study cultured human and animal cell lines, including those derived from cancer tissue, and analyze human and/or animal tissues with and without cancer. Perform sophisticated genetic, biological, and biochemical assays, including DNA transfection, monolayer and anchorage-independent growth assays, immunoprecipitation, agarose gel electrophoresis, and in vivo and in vitro kinase assays. Perform confocal immunofluorescence/confocal microscopy of cells. Plan, design, and perform experiments with laboratory personnel to support the laboratory's research program, including data analysis and interpretation. Initiate, design, and develop new research projects and assays supporting cancer research, diagnostics, therapeutics, and vaccine development. Serve as a subject matter resource on tumor biology and laboratory assays. Evaluate experimental results and methodological limitations; recommend adjustments, additional investigations, or changes in research direction based on findings and program objectives. Conduct in vitro and in vivo analyses to evaluate the effects of novel experimental therapeutics on immune cells, cancer cells, and tumors grown in mice. Support research involving mechanisms of cell transformation, molecular targets, and biomarkers with potential applications in cancer prevention, diagnosis, and treatment. Collaborate with scientists, statisticians, and other research professionals across NCI, NIH, and extramural institutions to clarify research needs, objectives, and experimental approaches. Evaluate, analyze, interpret, and communicate research data through technical reports, presentations, manuscripts, tables, and figures. Prepare scientific data and materials for collaborative and first-author publications in peer-reviewed journals. Present research findings at scientific conferences, seminars, meetings, and other forums. Attend scientific seminars and training opportunities and participate in research discussions, committees, panels, and colloquia. Work with mice and human blood or other body fluids while following laboratory safety procedures, universal precautions, personal protective equipment requirements, and biological material disposal requirements. What You Will Need: Master's degree in Biology, Life Sciences, or a related scientific discipline. Minimum of TWO (2) years of experience in a laboratory environment. Research background in immunology and/or molecular biology, translational cancer research or cancer drug development, and in vitro and in vivo cancer studies. Expertise in handling mice, including intraperitoneal and intravenous injections, tumor measurements, and dissections; ability to work with human blood and other body fluids. Proficiency in immunologic studies, including multiparameter flow cytometry processing and data analysis. Proficiency in in vitro functional assays, including cell culture, stimulation, proliferation, and cytokine production. Expertise in DNA/RNA extraction and quantification, gel electrophoresis, Western blotting, PCR, and immunoprecipitation. Ability to independently manage research projects, analyze findings, troubleshoot experiments, and develop new laboratory assays or research methodologies. Knowledge of laboratory techniques, safety procedures, universal precautions, and disposal methods Strong written and oral scientific communication skills, including manuscript preparation for peer-reviewed journals and presentations at conferences or seminars. Proficiency in Microsoft Word, PowerPoint, and Excel. Hands-on skills in confocal microscopy. Must be able to obtain and maintain a Federal or DoD “public trust”; candidates must receive approved adjudication prior to onboarding with Guidehouse. Candidates with an active public trust or suitability are preferred. What Would Be Nice To Have: Ph.D. in Biology, Life Sciences, or a related discipline preferred. Ability to learn new laboratory techniques and train others in established procedures. Strong analytical, organizational, interpersonal, and time-management skills, with the ability to collaborate closely with laboratory and scientific personnel.
