Discover more about our company-wide benefits and life at GSK on our webpage Life at GSK | GSK Find out more about GSK and R&D: Our approach to R&D Senior AI/ML Engineer, Human Physiology Modeling GSK is building a computational capability that models human physiology as an interconnected, multiscale system. We are hiring the founding member of a new team. You will set the technical direction, build the first models and help define the team's practices. You will also connect models across scales, from molecular and cellular to tissue, organ and whole-body level and turn that scope into a prioritized modeling roadmap, choosing the right method for each question. You will work with key stakeholders across GSK. The goal is models that inform real decisions, such as target hypotheses, mechanism of action, safety, biomarkers and patient stratification. This is a hands-on role. You will write, review and ship production-quality scientific software, as well as set the scientific direction. This is a hybrid role in the United States in San Francisco, CA or Cambridge, MA. You will be expected to work on-site regularly and collaborate with colleagues across sites and regions in the United States. This role will provide YOU the opportunity to lead key activities to progress YOUR career. These responsibilities include some of the following : Define and own the modeling strategy and phased roadmap across the team's organ systems. Recommend sequencing based on scientific tractability, data availability and portfolio relevance. Build, calibrate and validate multiscale models of physiological function, regulation and disease. Define how models of different organ systems and scales interface and compose. Integrate heterogeneous data into models: human genetics and genomics, single-cell and spatial omics, imaging, clinical and EHR data, and published physiology knowledge. Establish standards for uncertainty quantification, sensitivity analysis, parameter identifiability and model validation. Write reproducible, tested, version-controlled and containerized code that runs on GSK cloud and HPC infrastructure, in partnership with platform engineering. Work with experimental colleagues to design studies that test or constrain model predictions. Communicate results and their limits clearly to scientists and senior leaders. Help hire, onboard and mentor future team members. Evangelize and enforce code-review, documentation and modeling standards set by the group. Track the external literature and open-source ecosystem, and contribute or publish where appropriate. Basic Qualifications: We are looking for professionals with these required skills to achieve our goals: A minimum of a BS, MS, or PhD in computational or systems biology, biomedical engineering, physics, applied mathematics, computer science or a related field. A minimum of 4 years of experience with a PhD, a minimum of 5 years of experience with an MS and a minimum of 8 years of experience with a BS. All experience must involve building and applying quantitative models of biological or physiological systems, in academia or industry. For candidates without an advanced degree, a track record of published work, shipped models or open-source contributions can substitute for some of the required years. A minimum of 2 years of experience modeling the physical and biological processes that recur across organ systems. These include fluid flow and transport, mass exchange across barriers and membranes, tissue mechanics, hormonal and neural regulation, and cell population dynamics. A minimum of 4 years of experience with Advanced Python Programming. You can write robust, tested, maintainable scientific code using version control, CI/CD and containerization. A minimum of 2 years of modeling human physiology. A minimum of 2 years of taking models from concept to a result that changed a scientific or program decision. Preferred Qualifications: Please note the following skills are not necessary, just preferred, if you do not have them, please still apply: Experience with organ-level or whole-body modeling, including physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) and quantitative systems pharmacology (QSP) models. Familiarity with open-source simulation tools and standards (for example PhysiCell, COPASI, OpenCOR, FEniCS, SBML and CellML) and with HPC/GPU workflows. Experience with differentiable simulation, physics-informed ML, learned world models or generative models of biological systems. Experience with LLM and agentic tooling for scientific workflows is also useful. Experience with Kubernetes, workflow orchestration and MLOps on cloud or HPC. Publications in computational biology, systems biology or ML venues, or open-source contributions.
