Responsibilities Support development of predictive modeling and simulation workflows for CHO cell culture and upstream bioprocess digital twin applications. Build, test, and evaluate models for key upstream process behaviors, including nutrient consumption, metabolite accumulation, cell growth, viability, and productivity. Analyze bioprocess datasets, including time-series bioreactor data, offline assay results, spent media data, and process metadata. Use Python and scientific computing tools to process data, run simulations, visualize results, and support model evaluation. Collaborate with scientists, engineers, and software developers to summarize modeling results, document assumptions, and support experimental planning. Education Master's or Ph.D’s Degree in Biochemical Engineering, Chemical Engineering, Biotechnology, Computational Biology, Systems Biology, Bioinformatics, Cell Biology, or a related field. Qualifications & Experience Relevant academic or industry experience in upstream bioprocessing, cell culture, process modeling, computational biology, data science, or related scientific domains. Good understanding of CHO cell culture and upstream bioprocessing concepts, including media, feeding strategies, bioreactor operation, metabolites, cell growth, viability, productivity, and process parameters. Hands-on Python experience for scientific computing, data analysis, visualization, simulation, or model implementation. Experience working with bioprocess or biological datasets, especially time-series data, assay results, spent media data, or process metadata. Familiarity with mechanistic, statistical, machine learning, or hybrid modeling approaches for biological or process systems. Strong analytical and problem-solving skills, with attention to data quality and scientific detail. Ability to work effectively in cross-functional teams and contribute to project activities under guidance from senior scientists or project leads. Preferred Familiarity with PAT systems, bioreactor data infrastructure, LIMS/ELN/MES environments, cloud-based data workflows, or software-enabled scientific tools is a plus. Exposure to digital twin modeling, virtual experimentation, metabolic modeling, or process simulation is a plus.
