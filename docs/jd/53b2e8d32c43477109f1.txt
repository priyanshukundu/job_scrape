About Paxos

Today’s financial infrastructure is archaic, expensive, inefficient and risky — supporting a system that leaves out more people than it lets in. So we’re rebuilding it.

We’re on a mission to open the world’s financial system to everyone by enabling the instant movement of any asset, any time, in a trustworthy way. For over a decade, we’ve built blockchain infrastructure that tokenizes, custodies, trades and settles assets for the world's leading financial institutions, like PayPal, Venmo, Mastercard, Interactive Brokers and Charles Schwab.


ABOUT THE TEAM

The Liquidity team builds the infrastructure that moves money into, out of, and across the Paxos ecosystem. It spans three areas: fiat rails, orchestration rails, and core treasury systems. Together they let clients move in and out of stablecoins like USDG and PYUSD quickly, safely, and at a predictable price. That means integrating with banks and real-time payment rails worldwide, routing between fiat and stablecoins across venues and blockchains, and running the mint, burn, wallet, and reserve systems a regulated issuer depends on. Our goal is fully automated, 24/7 liquidity that settles large transactions in minutes, not days, and meets regulated-institution compliance standards. Our work powers issuance, payments, and brokerage for some of the world's largest financial and fintech companies. As the industry shifts from competing on "whose coin" to "whose network," Liquidity is at the center of how Paxos competes.


ABOUT THE ROLE

As a Senior Software Engineer on the Liquidity team, you'll own major parts of the systems that move money between banks, blockchains, and trading venues for Paxos and its clients. This is a high-ownership role. Rather than picking up tickets, you'll take projects from an ambiguous problem to production. You'll write the technical design, break the work into milestones, estimate and sequence it, and drive it to a committed date. You'll be the point of contact for your projects, coordinating with product, Treasury, Operations, Compliance, and partner engineering teams. That means surfacing risks and dependencies early, making tradeoff calls when scope or timelines shift, and sharing clear status updates without waiting to be asked.
