Director, AI & Emerging Technology Risk

The Director, AI & Emerging Technology Risk is a hands-on leader responsible for establishing and evolving how the enterprise identifies, assesses, and mitigates cyber risk from artificial intelligence (AI), large language models (LLMs), post-quantum cryptography (PQC), and other emerging technologies. Reporting to the Vice President, Cybersecurity Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC), this role sets the cyber risk strategy, risk assessment methodology, and security control expectations for AI and emerging technologies and personally delivers high-impact assessments and technical reviews. This role combines deep technical credibility with risk expertise. The Director leads/co-leads AI and LLM security risk assessments, security-relevant model risk practices, and the enterprise PQC risk and prioritization strategy, and works closely with AI Governance, security engineering, architecture, data science, legal, privacy, cyber risk and enterprise risk management (ERM) to enable secure innovation. Responsibilities: AI & Emerging Technology Risk Strategy Define and lead the strategy for AI risk and emerging technology risk management, aligned with enterprise risk management frameworks, business objectives, and regulatory expectations. Partner with the VP, Cybersecurity GRC to set risk priorities and risk appetite for AI, quantum, and other emerging technologies, while maintaining a forward-looking view of emerging threats, regulations, and technology shifts with defined triggers for escalation. Serve as a trusted advisor to senior leadership on AI and emerging technology risk posture, tradeoffs, and mitigation strategies. Define and maintain KPIs and KRIs for AI risk, model risk, and PQC readiness, and develop executive-level reporting that translates technical AI and quantum risks into clear business impact and actionable decisions. AI Risk Frameworks & Governance Partnership Design and maintain the AI cyber risk assessment framework, risk tiering criteria, and security control requirements, aligned with NIST AI RMF, OWASP, and MITRE ATLAS, and provide risk and security inputs to AI Governance on enterprise AI policy, ISO/IEC 42001, and EU AI Act readiness. Provide risk tiering criteria and security risk assessments that feed the AI Governance use-case intake and approval process, enabling the business to adopt AI safely and at speed. Integrate AI and emerging technology risks into the enterprise cyber risk taxonomy, risk register, and exception processes, serving as the cyber risk counterpart to AI Governance, Legal, Privacy, Compliance, and Data Science. Track emerging AI threats, standards, and regulations, partner with Legal, Compliance, and AI Governance on regulatory interpretation, and provide risk evidence for audit and regulatory inquiries. Securing AI & LLM Systems Lead security risk assessments of AI/ML and LLM solutions, including generative AI, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and agentic AI use cases, whether built, bought, or embedded in third-party products. Assess and drive mitigation of AI-specific threats such as prompt injection, jailbreaks, sensitive data leakage, training data and model poisoning, model theft, insecure tool or plugin use, and excessive agent autonomy. Define security control requirements across the AI lifecycle, including data sourcing, training, deployment, access management, monitoring, logging, and decommissioning. Partner with security engineering to implement and validate guardrails, AI red teaming, and adversarial testing. AI Model & Operational Risk Partner with AI Governance and enterprise model risk management to define the security and resilience risk criteria applied to the AI/ML model inventory, risk classification, validation, ongoing monitoring, and change management. Assess security- and resilience-relevant model risks, such as poisoning, drift, hallucination, reliability, and misuse, and ensure accountable owners and compensating controls are in place. Fairness, bias, and explainability remain with AI Governance and Responsible AI. Align AI risk processes with enterprise model governance and regulatory expectations in a highly regulated environment, including issues, exceptions, and risk acceptance processes to ensure appropriate documentation, approval, and alignment with defined risk tolerance levels. Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) & Quantum Risk Own the enterprise quantum risk assessment and risk-based prioritization of cryptographic exposure, including cryptographic inventory and discovery, and set requirements for and track progress against the phased PQC migration roadmap. Drive crypto-agility across applications, infrastructure, PKI, and third-party products, accounting for “harvest now, decrypt later” exposure for long-lived sensitive data. Track NIST PQC standards, including FIPS 203, 204, and 205, and regulatory guidance, translating them into enterprise standards and engineering requirements. Partner with architecture, cryptography, PKI, and engineering teams on testing, pilots, rollout, and quantum-readiness expectations for vendors. Emerging Technology & Third-Party AI Risk Evaluate the risks of other emerging technologies as they arise (e.g., agentic AI platforms, AI-enabled SaaS, and next-generation cloud services) and define proportionate control expectations. Partner with the cyber third-party risk management (TPRM) program to add AI-specific due diligence and support ongoing monitoring of AI-enabled vendors, including model provenance, data handling, use of customer data for training, embedded AI features, quantum readiness, and appropriate security, data protection, and transparency requirements. Engineering & Business Partnership Partner with security architecture, engineering, data science, and IT teams to embed risk requirements into AI and technology design, the SDLC, and MLOps pipelines. Provide hands-on technical guidance, design reviews, and threat modeling for high-risk AI and cryptography-dependent initiatives. Collaborate with Cybersecurity, IT, AI Governance, Legal, Compliance, Audit, and business teams to enable secure and resilient AI adoption, embed risk practices into enterprise operations, and support audit and regulatory activities through risk documentation, insights, and remediation tracking. Qualifications: 10+ years of experience in cybersecurity, technology risk, security engineering, or GRC, including recent hands-on experience with AI or emerging technology risk. Engineering background (software, security, systems, or cloud engineering) with a BS or MS in Computer Science, Engineering, or a related technical field. Practical experience securing AI and LLM systems, including threat modeling, risk assessment, and control design for generative AI, RAG, and agentic use cases (e.g., prompt injection, data leakage, model poisoning). Experience with post-quantum risk, including cryptographic inventory, crypto-agility, NIST PQC standards, and migration planning. Working knowledge of AI/ML risk management, including model inventory, validation, and monitoring, and of security- and resilience-relevant model risks such as drift, poisoning, and misuse. Familiarity with AI and security frameworks and standards (e.g., NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST CSF, ISO 27001, OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, MITRE ATLAS) and emerging AI regulations. Hands-on experience with AI red teaming, adversarial testing, or building and securing ML pipelines and cloud AI platforms. Experience with applied cryptography, PKI, or cryptographic discovery and migration programs. Experience in highly regulated industries (e.g., healthcare, life sciences, financial services, or government). Professional certifications such as CISSP, CISM, CRISC, CISA, CCSP, or AI governance and security credentials (e.g., AIGP, ISACA AAISM) preferred. Experience developing executive-level reporting and communicating complex technology risk to senior leadership, with strong cross-functional stakeholder management skills.
